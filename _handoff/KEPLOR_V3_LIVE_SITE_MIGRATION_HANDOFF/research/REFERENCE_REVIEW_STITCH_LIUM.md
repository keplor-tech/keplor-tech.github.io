# Keplor Design Reference Review — Google Stitch + Lium

**Reviewed:** 24 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Incorporate useful design-process and product-storytelling lessons into the approved Keplor Canva-aligned design without changing the chosen visual direction.

## Executive decision

Keep `prototype/FINAL-APPROVED-FULL-PAGE-MOCKUP.png` as the canonical page-composition reference. The current chosen direction remains a minimal, spacious, near-black website with large sans-serif typography and broad, out-of-focus blue/cyan/violet/magenta light fields. Do not replace it with literal space objects, planets, orbs, rings, particle simulations, dense card systems, or a new visual theme.

Use the two new references for different jobs:

- **Google Stitch:** a design-process and design-system reference. Its official product writing describes natural-language/mood-driven design, a persistent canvas for exploring multiple directions, an agent-friendly `DESIGN.md`, connected interactive prototypes, and iterative critique. Apply that methodology to Keplor's design handoff and refinement workflow, not as a visual style to copy.
- **Lium (`lium.ai`):** a content architecture and positioning reference. Its live homepage leads with a specific benefit, explains the product through a sequence of named capabilities, brings the story into domain-specific use cases, shows a featured study, answers common questions, and closes with a repeated CTA. Adapt the clarity and progression to Keplor's wider services business; do not copy its product claims or its self-serve CTA.

## 1. Google Stitch — what was verified

Sources:
- https://stitch.withgoogle.com/
- https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/stitch-ai-ui-design/
- https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/stitch-updates/

Google's official articles describe Stitch as an AI-native design canvas that generates and iterates high-fidelity UI from natural language and visual context. The March 2026 article describes an infinite canvas, design-system exchange through an agent-friendly `DESIGN.md`, interactive prototypes and connected flows, real-time design critique, and ways to export work to development tools. Google's May 2026 update describes real-time collaboration, steering iterations, and export toward Google AI Studio, Antigravity or Netlify.

### What Keplor should borrow

1. **One durable design system:** maintain a single source of truth for colors, typography, spacing, content width, motion, component states, responsive rules, and prohibited patterns. The included `DESIGN.md` serves this role for the coding agent.
2. **Visual iteration instead of one-shot prompting:** review the desktop hero, full-page layout, mobile page and motion sequence as separate artifacts; refine them against the supplied original Canva references.
3. **Prototype actual journeys:** all nav items, in-page anchors, contact form states and service/industry links should lead somewhere real. A visual click-through is not proof a backend exists.
4. **Use design references as context, not as a license to copy:** inspect the inspiration, extract principles, then create original Keplor composition and copy.
5. **Converge on the approved design:** explore alternatives only when there is a concrete unresolved issue; do not let an agent spontaneously regenerate the aesthetic on every iteration.

### What not to infer

Stitch is an AI design tool, not a single website template. Its product features do not mean Keplor should resemble Stitch's workspace UI. Its value here is design-system discipline and iterative validation.

## 2. Lium — live website content and architecture observations

Sources:
- https://www.lium.ai/
- https://www.lium.ai/platform
- https://www.lium.ai/about

The current public homepage describes Lium as “AI for real-world data work” and introduces it with a concise benefit statement. It then presents a coherent capability sequence: “Connect Everything,” “Operationalize Your Data,” “Analyze and Create,” “Compute on Demand,” and “Build on Everything.” It moves into domains/industries, a featured study, common questions, a repeated CTA and a compact footer. The platform page explains a system in layers and describes how those layers relate. These are content/IA observations from the publicly accessible pages, not claims that Keplor has the same product or architecture.

### What Keplor should borrow

1. **A clear opening proposition:** lead with the business value and identify the work Keplor helps with in the supporting sentence. Avoid abstract AI slogans that leave the visitor unsure what the company does.
2. **A narrative, not a catalogue:** move from services → relevant industries/problems → approach → mission/vision → contact. Each section should answer one question and set up the next.
3. **Capability names that say what happens:** keep Keplor's actual service pillars, but write descriptions in plain business language. Technical breadth can appear in a deeper level, not all in the hero.
4. **Domain relevance:** the five industries in Keplor's company blueprint should be easy to scan and relate to their stated use cases. Do not imply proven deployments in those sectors unless Keplor can substantiate them.
5. **A repeatable CTA:** keep a consistent “Discuss a project” / “Let's talk” path through the page. Lium's “Try for Free” CTA is appropriate to its product; Keplor should use a consultation/contact CTA because the current direction is a services-led company.
6. **Proof only when real:** Lium's public page presents a featured study and measured results. Keplor currently has no live customer projects per founder-provided context, so replace this pattern with a useful explanation of the approach, areas of capability, or a clear discovery conversation—not invented metrics, case studies or customer logos.
7. **Handle detail progressively:** keep the homepage restrained; put the complete AI/ML, software, cloud and research/analytics detail on the Services page or inside accessible sections.

### What not to copy

- Do not claim Lium's feature set, scale, customer relationships, domain expertise, metrics, security posture or results for Keplor.
- Do not copy product-specific labels or text.
- Do not adopt “Try for Free” or fake an application/product UI if Keplor is currently offering business solutions and project consultations.
- Do not add unverified client logos or an imitation of Lium's customer study as if Keplor had one.

## 3. Final combined direction for Keplor

### Visual authority

The five founder-supplied Canva references and the approved full-page mockup remain the visual authority. The visual is the blurred light field itself—not an object in space. Use a near-black base, generous negative space, large contemporary sans-serif display text, sparse navigation, hairline dividers, restrained buttons and broadly diffused light.

### Storytelling authority

Use Lium as a reference for clarity and a progressive, named sequence of capabilities. Preserve Keplor's supplied company content:

1. Hero — clear promise and service scope.
2. Services — AI/ML, custom software, cloud, research & analytics.
3. Industries — healthcare, finance, education, retail/e-commerce, manufacturing.
4. Approach — understand, design, build, improve.
5. About — official mission and vision.
6. Contact — a clear path to discuss a project.

### Process authority

Use Stitch as a reference for a repeatable, design-system-driven iteration process. Read `DESIGN.md`, compare the actual browser screenshots against the approved mockups, make specific refinements, and re-check the result.
